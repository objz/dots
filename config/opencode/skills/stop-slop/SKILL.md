# Stop Slop

Apply to every piece of prose before delivering it. Cutting never changes a number, price, date, name, URL, quoted text, code or a negation. In sales copy written under the copywriting skill, its deliberate devices (open loops, threshold questions, either-or closes) win; remove the filler around them.

1. Filler: cut throat-clearing openers ("Here's the thing", "It turns out"), emphasis crutches ("Let that sink in") and intensifier or hedge adverbs (really, just, actually, genuinely). Adverbs that carry information (not, now, only, already) stay. Read [references/phrases.md](references/phrases.md) when scanning a draft for filler.
2. Formula: no "not X, it's Y" contrasts, no negative listing, no staccato fragments, no "What if" or "Here's what I mean" setups. State Y. Read [references/structures.md](references/structures.md) when a paragraph reads formulaic.
3. Actor: name who acts. Abstractions do not act ("the decision emerges"); a system that acts ("the webhook writes a row") keeps its verb. A passive stays only when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
4. Specifics: no vague declaratives ("The implications are significant"); name the implication. No lazy extremes (every, always, never) doing vague work; a true absolute stays.
5. Reader: address the reader as "you" instead of describing "people" from a distance.
6. Rhythm: mix sentence lengths and paragraph endings. Prefer two items to a rhetorical triad; a list of three real things stays. No em dashes, and no en dashes or hyphens standing in for them.
7. Trust: state facts without softening or justifying them. Delete meta-joiners ("The rest of this essay...").
8. Quotables: if a sentence sounds like a pull-quote, rewrite it.

Read [references/examples.md](references/examples.md) when unsure what a rewrite should look like.
